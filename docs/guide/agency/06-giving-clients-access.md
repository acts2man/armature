# Giving clients access

## Two ways to add a client

Open **Clients → Add a client** (or a site's **Users** tab):

- **Create a login yourself.** You set a temporary password and Armature builds a
  ready-to-send message with the sign-in link, the email address, and the
  password. The client must choose their own password before they can do anything
  else. Use this when you want to hand a client credentials in person or over a
  channel they trust.
- **Invite by link.** Armature emails the client a one-time link that works for
  seven days. When email is not set up, the dashboard shows the link so you can
  copy it into whichever channel you use.

## Editing levels

On the site's overview, choose what the client can do inside the editor:

- **Content** — the words and pictures on the page. Nothing added, moved or
  removed; no restyle. Safest for a client who just wants to keep their
  headlines fresh.
- **Style** — content plus the Style tab (colours, sizes, borders, shadows).
  Still no adding or moving.
- **Builder** — the full page builder: add sections, drag things around, use every
  widget.

Any element you lock keeps its words editable for clients (they can still fix a
typo) but stays where you put it and looks the way you chose.

## View as client

On the Clients screen, **View as** opens the site exactly as that person sees
it — the client menu, the client screens, read-only. Every write is refused in
the browser before it leaves, so your own session and rights are never touched.
The banner across the top says whose view it is; Exit ends the view and takes
you back to Clients.

## Requests

Clients ask for anything they cannot do themselves through the site's **Requests**
menu (also on the client's dashboard as "Need something bigger?"). Requests come
in with a title, a note, screenshots and the page they were made from. Agency
staff can move a request through New / In progress / Ready for review / Done or
declined, add internal notes, and reply.
